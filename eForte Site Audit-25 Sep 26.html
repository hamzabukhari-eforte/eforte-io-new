Verdict: do not point eforte.io at this build yet

The engineering is sound. Every route returns 200, there are no console errors, no broken images, no failed requests, and the design system is consistent across 101 pages. That is a better starting position than most site rebuilds reach.

The blockers are content and claims, not code. Thirty-seven of the forty-eight case studies describe clients that do not appear to exist, complete with invented product domains and precise performance figures — and one of them is the featured study on your homepage. The homepage logo strip presents Google, Slack, PayPal, Shopify, Figma, Ripple and AWS under the heading "Trusted by industry leaders." Separately, robots.txt currently blocks every crawler including Googlebot, so if that file ships as-is the site will not be indexed at all.

Fix the four criticals before launch. The high-severity items are a week of focused work and mostly mechanical.

CriticalC-01robots.txt · indexing

robots.txt blocks every crawler, including Googlebot

The deployed /robots.txt disallows all of / for seventeen named agents plus the wildcard. If this file reaches production unchanged, eforte.io will be entirely absent from Google, Bing, and every AI crawler on day one.

User-Agent: *          Disallow: /
User-Agent: Googlebot  Disallow: /
User-Agent: GPTBot     Disallow: /
User-Agent: Bingbot    Disallow: /
… 13 more agents, all Disallow: /

This is very likely intentional for a Vercel preview build. But the sitemap it ships alongside already points at https://eforte.io, which tells me the robots file and the sitemap are generated by different logic — and only one of them knows it is a preview.

Fix: make robots generation read VERCEL_ENV and emit Allow: / only on production. Then verify the live file on eforte.io within an hour of cutover, before requesting indexing in Search Console.
CriticalC-02content integrity · legal

37 of 48 case studies describe clients that do not appear to exist

Brightwell Mutual, Cobalt Digital Bank, Harborlight Recovery, Fenwick Capital Markets, Thornfield Health, Velora Financial, Arclight Capital and thirty more follow an identical pattern: a plausible but unverifiable company name, a bespoke product domain, and four precise performance figures. They are presented in the same template, with the same visual weight, as Krank and Oddysee — which are real, named, traceable clients.

/case-studies/brightwell-mutual
  "eForte delivered member.brightwellmutual.com"
  First-contact resolution 78%  ·  Cost per case −24%
  71% simple claims STP         ·  Retention 93%

The same page also carries two incompatible descriptions of the client. The opening paragraph calls Brightwell a regional insurer with a manual claims-review bottleneck; six lines later it is a mutual serving members across complex benefits plans whose care agents lack next-best actions. Two different content sets are rendering into one template.

The commercial exposure is direct. A prospect who searches "Brightwell Mutual eForte" finds nothing. A competitor who notices the pattern has a ready-made story. And in regulated verticals — you are claiming zero compliance findings in audits for a named life-sciences client — invented results are the kind of thing procurement and legal teams escalate rather than query.

Fix: remove all 37 from the sitemap and unpublish them before launch. If they are needed to make the industry pages feel populated, relabel the whole set as illustrative scenarios, strip the invented domains, and present the figures as modelled ranges rather than client outcomes. Keep the 11 real studies as the case-study library.
CriticalC-03homepage · brand claims

"Trusted by industry leaders" shows logos of companies that are not clients

The homepage logo strip, directly beneath the hero, renders Land id., Figma, Ripple, Google, Shopify, OneSignal, Slack, PayPal and AWS under that heading. The assets are committed at /assets/final-images/brands/, so these are deliberate, not a placeholder library left in by accident.

/assets/final-images/brands/ripple.svg
/assets/final-images/brands/shopify.svg
/assets/final-images/brands/onesignal.svg
/assets/final-images/brands/landid.svg   → 304, all rendering

Using a company's registered wordmark to imply a client relationship that does not exist is the clearest trademark exposure on the site, and every one of these companies has a brand-enforcement function. It also undercuts the real proof you have: you genuinely do have nine named client reviews with attributable people and domains, and those are more persuasive to a medtech or financial-services buyer than a Google logo ever was.

Fix: replace with the logos of clients who have given written permission, or drop the strip and promote the reviews carousel into that slot. If AWS, Microsoft and Anthropic relationships are formal partner-programme memberships, move those marks to a "Technology partners" strip with the programme name stated, which is what those programmes permit.
CriticalC-04certifications · verification

Seven certification and award claims, none linked to evidence

The homepage and /databricks between them assert: SOC 2 Type 2, ISO 27001, NIST AI RMF compliance, IBM Global Entrepreneur (two consecutive years), an Expertise.com "A" rating, CAIDP membership, HIPAA compliance expertise, and Databricks Gold Partner status. None carries a certificate number, issuing body, audit date, or verification link.

Two are worth checking specifically. SOC 2 Type 2 is the strongest claim on the page — the copy says it "verifies that eForte maintains stringent security protocols over an extended period of time," which asserts a completed observation window and a named auditor. And "Databricks Gold Partner" does not match the tier names Databricks uses for consulting partners; if the correct tier is different, the page is wrong in a way a Databricks-literate buyer will catch immediately.

Fix: for each claim, either attach the certificate, report date and auditor, or remove it. Trust pages are read most carefully by exactly the buyers you want. One verifiable ISO certificate outperforms seven unverifiable badges.
HighH-01SEO · metadata

All 48 case-study pages share one title and one description

Every route under /case-studies/ returns <title>eForte Solutions</title> and the site-wide fallback description. That is 48 pages — nearly half the site — that Google will treat as duplicates and largely drop from the index.

/case-studies/krank             → "eForte Solutions"
/case-studies/oddysee           → "eForte Solutions"
/case-studies/investment-markets → "eForte Solutions"
… 45 more, identical title and meta description
Fix: add generateMetadata to the case-study route. Title as {client} Case Study | eForte; description from the existing challenge paragraph, trimmed to 155 characters.
HighH-02SEO · metadata

Two of your three AI pillars have no page metadata

/velocity-ai and /agentic-orchestration both fall back to "eForte Solutions" and the generic description, while /foundational-data-layer has proper metadata. These are your three named differentiators, repeated in nearly every CTA on the site, and two of them are invisible in search results.

Fix: copy the metadata pattern already working on /foundational-data-layer. Fifteen minutes of work.
HighH-03performance · LCP

Hero headline renders at opacity 0 for several seconds after load

The homepage H1 and its supporting paragraph mount with opacity: 0 and a translateY offset, and only resolve to full opacity after a delay. Measured in the pane: still at 0 four seconds after the load event, fully visible by ten. For that window the hero is an empty gradient.

h1  computed opacity: 0   transform: matrix(1,0,0,1,0,16)
    parent opacity: 0     transform: matrix(1,0,0,1,0,24)
→ measured again after ~7s: opacity 1

Two consequences. The H1 is almost certainly your Largest Contentful Paint element, and an element hidden until it animates in reports a delayed LCP — a direct Core Web Vitals penalty. And a visitor arriving on a slow connection sees a blank hero, which is the moment they decide whether to stay.

Fix: render the hero text visible at rest and animate only a subtle transform, or cap the entrance delay at roughly 300ms. Also honour prefers-reduced-motion, which the current implementation does not. Confirm with Lighthouse on the production domain — the preview pane may exaggerate the delay.
HighH-04SEO · social

No Open Graph or Twitter Card tags on any page

Checked all 101 routes: zero og: or twitter: properties. Every link shared to LinkedIn, Slack, WhatsApp or X will render as a bare URL with no title, description or image. For a B2B services business where LinkedIn is the primary distribution channel, this removes the preview card from every post you or your team will ever make.

Fix: add openGraph and twitter blocks to the root metadata with a default 1200×630 card, then override per template. Next.js opengraph-image.tsx can generate case-study and blog cards automatically from the page data.
HighH-05SEO · duplication

No canonical tags, and one route duplicates another

No page declares <link rel="canonical">. Meanwhile /case-studies serves the identical page as /impact-studies, and the Vercel preview domain is itself a full duplicate of the production site once eforte.io goes live.

Fix: set metadataBase and a self-referencing canonical in root metadata. Redirect /case-studies to /impact-studies with a 301. Keep the preview domain's blanket robots block so it never competes with production.
HighH-06SEO · structured data

No structured data anywhere on the site

Zero application/ld+json blocks across 101 pages. No Organization schema, no Article schema on the eight blog posts, no BreadcrumbList, no JobPosting on the two open roles. This is also the single highest-leverage fix for AI answer engines, which lean heavily on structured data to decide what an organisation is and does.

Fix, in priority order: Organization + WebSite schema sitewide (name, logo, sameAs, contactPoint); Article on blog posts with author and datePublished; JobPosting on role pages, which makes them eligible for the Google Jobs panel; BreadcrumbList on the nested templates.

Three pages give three different answers about where eForte is

There is no version of this company's geography that a reader can construct from the site.

/about-us   Wilmington DE · San Jose CA · Austin TX · remote/nearshore
/locations  Austin · Chicago · Nashville · New York · Montevideo · Cali
/careers/associate-dotnet-developer
            "Join the Karachi engineering team"

Karachi — where the engineering actually happens — appears only inside one job description and on no locations list at all. Meanwhile /locations adds Uruguay and Colombia offices that no other page acknowledges, and its Chicago entry reads NIL 60654 where the state code should be IL.

HighH-09content · consistency

/databricks contradicts the rest of the site on company scale and history

The page claims "20+ years of experience working with data" and "250+ professionals certified on the Databricks Intelligence platform." /about-us dates the founding to 2013 and describes passing 40 engineers in 2017. The homepage and about page both say "over a decade."

It also announces a Databricks specialisation for "Communications, Media, Entertainment & Gaming" — a vertical absent from the site's seven industries and from every other page. Combined with the tier-name issue in C-04, this page reads as adapted from another company's site and never reconciled.

Fix: rewrite against real figures, or take the page down. As written it undermines the numbers on every other page a prospect reads.
HighH-10conversion · untested

The contact modal is the only conversion path, and it is unverified

Every CTA on the site — "Get in touch", "Get a Quote", "Contact Us", "Let's build intelligence together" — opens the same modal. There is no contact page, no email address, and no phone number anywhere in the markup: zero mailto: and zero tel: links across 101 routes. If the modal's handler fails, the site has no way to reach eForte at all.

form action: null  (JS handler)
  name         text      required   labelled
  email        email     required   labelled
  phone        tel       optional   labelled
  projectType  hidden    required   NOT labelled   ← custom select
  description  textarea  optional   labelled
Modal promises: "consultant will call you in the next 24 hours"

I did not submit it — that would have sent a real enquiry into your pipeline. Two things I can see without submitting: projectType is a required hidden input backed by a custom dropdown, which is the classic shape for a form that silently fails validation with no visible error; and no input declares an autocomplete attribute, so browser autofill will not assist.

Fix: submit a test enquiry end to end and confirm where it lands, what the success and failure states look like, and whether the 24-hour promise is operationally real. Add a plain email address in the footer as a fallback. Add autocomplete="name | email | tel". Say the word and I will drive the submission with obvious test data.

MediumM-04English

Compound modifiers lose their hyphens on some pages but not others

The homepage writes "secure-first", "end-to-end", "AI-Augmented", "Human-in-the-Loop". Other pages write the same constructions with the hyphens stripped.

/technology-partners  AI native · hands on · enterprise grade
                      real time · human in the loop · time to market
/about-us             AI native · high quality · long term · follow through
/industries/healthcare  long term · hands on

vs. homepage: secure-first · end-to-end · AI-Augmented

Every one of these is a compound modifier before a noun and takes a hyphen. "Enterprise grade cloud" and "enterprise-grade cloud" mean different things; the first is barely grammatical. It reads as inconsistency rather than style, particularly to the US enterprise buyer the site targets.

Fix: one pass across /about-us, /technology-partners and the industry pages. Add the terms to a house style sheet so the next writer matches.
MediumM-05English

Reviews page heading says the opposite of what it means

The H1 on /reviews reads "Satisfactory reviews of clients." Reviews of clients would be eForte reviewing them. And "satisfactory" means adequate, barely passing — an odd word to choose for a page of five-star ratings.

Fix: "What our clients say" or "Client reviews". The meta description on the same page already gets it right.
MediumM-06content · attribution

MediumM-08responsive

18px of horizontal scroll at 375px width

At iPhone width the document scrolls sideways: scrollWidth 393 against a 375 viewport. The overflow traces to the fixed nav and the mobile menu container, which carry a scrollbar-width padding utility that resolves wider than the viewport on touch devices where there is no scrollbar to compensate for.

documentElement.clientWidth : 375
documentElement.scrollWidth : 393        ← 18px overflow
offending: NAV .fixed.top-0.left-0.right-0…pr-(--scrollbar…)
           + mobile menu .fixed.inset-0 (all width 393)

The mobile menu panel is also present in the DOM at full size while closed, so its links stay in the tab order when the menu is shut.

Fix: guard the scrollbar-width padding behind a pointer query, or set overflow-x: hidden on the wrapper. Give the closed mobile menu inert or visibility: hidden so it leaves the tab order.
MediumM-09accessibility

Eight unlabelled buttons on the homepage, and heavy empty-alt use

Eight of twenty-two buttons on the homepage have neither text content nor aria-label — a screen reader announces "button" with no further information. Eleven of twenty-eight images carry alt="", which is correct for decoration but wrong for the client logo strip, where the brand name is the content.

The pattern repeats across templates: /capabilities/cybersecurity has 13 images with no alt attribute out of 58, /capabilities/artificial-intelligence has 10 of 26.

MediumM-13English · logic

Things you did not ask about

Worth a decision
  • No analytics or tag manager fires on load. I saw no GA4, no Plausible, no LinkedIn Insight tag, no server-side events in 296 network requests. If you launch without measurement you will have no baseline for anything in this report, and no retargeting pool from the traffic you spend on acquiring.
  • No cookie consent mechanism. Not required today because nothing is tracking. The moment analytics goes in, a site that names Delaware and California offices and markets into healthcare and financial services needs a consent layer and a privacy policy that matches what actually runs.
  • The privacy policy and terms are substantial but unverified. 1,602 and 1,693 words respectively, well structured. Neither names the legal entity, a jurisdiction, or a contact for data requests — check both against the entity that actually signs your contracts.
  • The preview deployment will be a duplicate of production. Keep eforte-io-new.vercel.app blocked in robots permanently, and consider HTTP auth on it. Right now it is publicly readable by anyone with the URL, including the 37 case studies.
  • There is no thought-leadership route to Suricat. The site sells regulated-by-design AI, HIPAA expertise and compliance workflows, which is precisely the ground Suricat occupies.
